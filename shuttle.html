01 / GLIDE RATIO

The Hypersonic Brick

At Mach 25, the Orbiter has an atrocious ~1.0 Lift-to-Drag ratio. The high drag is intentionally engineered to convert immense kinetic energy into thermal energy far up in the upper atmosphere.

02 / ENERGY BLEED

S-Turn Roll Maneuvers

Without engines or throttle, the commander controls speed by banking the shuttle up to 80 degrees back and forth in wide S-turns to dissipate velocity while managing thermal tile heating.

03 / TRAJECTORY

Heading Alignment Cone

An imaginary overhead 360° spiral cylinder near the airfield (HAC). By circling this virtual cone, the unpowered glider bleeds off remaining altitude and lines up perfectly with the runway.

04 / TOUCHDOWN

The Steep Double Flare

Descent begins at a steep 20° angle (7x steeper than an airliner) at 300 knots. At 2,000 ft, the pilot pulls up into a shallow flare, dropping landing gear just 300 ft above the ground.

02 / Flight Profile Breakdown

From orbit to asphalt in 60 minutes.

Phase 1
400,000 FT · MACH 25

Entry Interface

OMS engines fire over the Indian Ocean. The Orbiter rolls nose-up to a 40° angle of attack, presenting its black ceramic heat shield to extreme plasma temperatures.

Phase 2
200,000 FT · MACH 15 - 5

Hypersonic Roll Banks

Ionized air causes a 12-minute communications blackout. The flight computer executes precision roll maneuvers to control downrange and crossrange distance.

Phase 3
80,000 FT · MACH 2.5 - SUBSONIC

TAEM & HAC Intercept

Reaction control thrusters shut down as aerodynamic rudders take over. The Shuttle intercepts the Heading Alignment Cone, spiraling around it overhead.

Phase 4
12,000 FT TO RUNWAY

Pre-Flare & Touchdown

Sinking at 10,000 ft/min on a 20° slope. The pilot executes the pre-flare at 2,000 ft, deploys main gear at 300 ft, and touches down at 210 knots.
